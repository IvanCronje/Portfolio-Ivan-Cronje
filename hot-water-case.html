Case study · Premier Resort Knysna, The Moorings · 2026

Hot water plant: from failing vessel to heat pumps

The main accommodation block's hot water storage vessel was leaking from its body and could not be repaired. This page follows the job from the condition report to the replacement plant, and says plainly which figures are documented, which are quotes, and which are not yet verified.

01 · The problem Documented

A vessel failing from the inside

During routine inspections the main hot water storage vessel was found to be losing water directly from the body of the tank, in the central section. The leak was not from pipework, fittings or valves, and the rate of loss was increasing. Corrosion was visible, and the pattern was consistent with long-term internal corrosion and age rather than a single event.

  • Repair was judged not viable: the failure is in the vessel itself, so routine plumbing maintenance could not fix it.
  • Risks written up: complete failure of the vessel, sudden water loss, interrupted hot water to guests, complaints, possible closure of rooms, damage to surrounding equipment, and lost revenue.
  • Stop-gap while options were priced: the existing heat pumps were repaired to keep a limited supply running. This did not address the vessel.

Framed as a compliance and trading risk

The case was built from first principles rather than from the failure alone: how much hot water the property needs against what it could store, how often the old boiler elements were failing, and what that meant for Legionella control and for selling rooms.

The plant was re-specified from the boiler quote to two 30 kW air-to-water heat pumps and two 2,500 litre accumulators, reusing an existing vessel, with Blygold coil protection against coastal salt air. On the quoted figures that is about R214,000 below the original quote for the required capacity. That gap compares two quotes. It is not a verified saving until scope, VAT and final costs are checked.

03 · Delivery Delivered

Installed around live occupancy

  • Supervised the full installation with the property trading, then commissioned the plant in 2026.
  • Handed over into daily temperature recording and monthly plant inspections.
  • Built an interactive simulation so the team could learn how the plant behaves before relying on it: three heat pumps in series feeding two accumulators, with occupancy, shower habits and a group-departure surge you can change.

04 · What is not yet proven Not yet verified

Limits

  • No operating, maintenance or energy-cost comparison between the old and new plant has been verified. The capital gap above is the only figure on this page that compares the two routes.
  • The simulation models heat pump performance from first principles, not from the manufacturer's published curves, so its outputs are indicative.
  • Guest-comfort and Legionella outcomes depend on the temperature records kept after handover. They are not claimed here.
