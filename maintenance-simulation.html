Run to failure versus planned

Simulate a set of identical assets over five years under three strategies: fix them when they break, service them on a fixed interval, or monitor condition and step in just before a failure. It shows how the answer depends on how assets fail and what a failure costs.

Model: each asset fails after a random time drawn from a Weibull distribution (a wear-out pattern above 1 means older means likelier to fail; 1 means failures are purely random). A planned service or intervention renews the asset to as-good-as-new. Planned work is assumed to be scheduled into quiet periods, so only failures carry lost revenue. Predictive monitoring is assumed to catch the stated share of failures 14 days ahead and to cost a fixed amount per asset per year. All inputs are illustrative: replace them with your own failure history and costs before using any result. 300 random runs per strategy, repeatable. Runs in your browser; nothing is saved or sent.
