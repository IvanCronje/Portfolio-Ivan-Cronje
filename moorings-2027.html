01 · The vision
To build a proactive, well-trained, guest-focused engineering department that keeps The Moorings running at the highest standard. We catch problems before they happen, we do the work ourselves, and we move the property towards energy and water independence.

Maintenance is not a support department. It is the department that enables the resort to operate. Every working light, hot shower, safe walkway and working kitchen lets another department succeed.

03 · The structure

Three teams, twenty-four hours

Three Team Leaders is the span one manager can supervise properly. Three to four technicians per team splits cleanly into buddy pairs and lets work be reassigned in seconds. Technicians rotate through all three teams, so there are no silos and nobody owns the night.

Team 01 · Day
Guest areas & rapid response
Rooms, public areas and guest requests: the fast-moving, guest-facing work. Lean: general repairs, plumbing, carpentry, finishes.
Team 02 · Day
Technical & infrastructure
Critical plant: heat pumps, boilers, solar, borehole, filtration, electrical. Owns diagnostics and planned servicing.
Team 03 · Night
Night & preventive
After-hours cover and preventive work best done while guests sleep: public areas, pool plant, kitchen equipment.

04 · How the work runs

Priority is set by a system, not by who shouts loudest

The Dynamic Priority Index runs on an in-house workbook: a settings sheet, a work order register and an asset register. Zero licence fees, zero subscriptions, and everything transfers into a commercial CMMS if the group ever chooses one.

Step 1
Scored on what it threatens
Revenue impact, failure severity, safety risk, operational impact and guest experience, less job complexity.
Step 2
Classified P1 to P7
P1 emergency, attend immediately. P2 urgent, same shift. P3 high, same day. P4 standard, this week. Then planned, scheduled, cosmetic.
Step 3
It keeps adjusting itself
A job open 30 days carries double its priority, capped at 3×. Jobs on critical assets get bumped up. Nothing is forgotten; nothing jumps the queue unfairly.

06 · At the guest's door

The room must look like we were never there

The only evidence is that the problem is gone. A completed-work card tells the guest we came, what was fixed and who to ask for. If a repair cannot be finished, a second card says so honestly, with a plan. Bad news with a plan builds more trust than silence. A job is finished only when the guest confirms it is right.

07 · The road to December 2027

09 · The Academy

The Moorings Engineering Academy

A structured 12 to 18 month curriculum, run like a flight academy but around the shifts, not instead of them. The property is the classroom: short lessons in the morning briefing, toolbox talks, real faults as training material. Progression is by competence, never by time served.

Level 01
Foundation
Safe tool use, hotel standards, basic electrical and plumbing, PM basics, DPI, safety induction.
Level 02
Skilled technician
Advanced fault-finding, AC, water systems, pumps, pool plant, root cause analysis.
Level 03
Specialist
HVAC, boilers, water treatment, refrigeration, controls. Specialists mentor.
Level 04
Leadership
Planning, coaching, budget, contractors, guest recovery, decisions under pressure.
