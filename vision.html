展望 · My vision for hotel engineering

Engineering is the invisible luxury

Where I want to take hotel engineering, and how I intend to help get it there. This vision belongs to me, not to any one property. It is what I carry from hotel to hotel and into the wider industry.

The belief
Hotel engineering should be a strategic discipline that shapes how hotels operate, how teams develop, how resources are used and how guests experience hospitality.

It should be more than maintaining buildings, repairing equipment and preventing failures. My vision is to help move the industry from reactive maintenance to intelligent, predictive and performance-driven engineering.

I want engineering departments recognised not merely as operational necessities but as strategic partners in a hotel's success: departments that understand how technical performance, financial sustainability, environmental responsibility and guest satisfaction connect.

The goal is simple: make engineering so effective that guests never have to think about it, teams are proud of it, and businesses understand its value. The greatest engineering achievement in hospitality is often the experience that makes the engineering itself invisible.

What I want to change

Five things I keep seeing

This is my view, from working in four-star, five-star and resort properties.

Today, too often
What I want instead
Success is measured by how fast a repair happens
Success is measured by what does not break: repeat failures prevented, root causes removed.
Engineering is a cost to be trimmed
Engineering is a strategic partner whose decisions are expressed in capital, operating cost, risk and guest impact.
Knowledge lives in a few heads, and handover is weak
Documented systems, asset records and standards, so the department performs when any one person is absent.
Training is counted by attendance
Training is proven by practical competence and the quality of work.
Sustainability and efficiency are claimed
They are measured, normalised for occupancy and conditions, and verified.
How I want to impact the industry

Six ways, in order of how far along they are

Each one is labelled honestly: In progress means I am doing it now, Proposal means it is designed but not adopted, Concept means it is an idea or a demonstration.

01In progress
Raise the standard
Publish a small library of original, reusable standards: an operating manual, a preventive maintenance standard, a risk and compliance framework, a competency framework and a monthly performance review. Blank templates and sanitised examples, written by me, for any department to use.
02In progress
Teach it
Build technicians and leaders, not just fixes. Competency defined before training, proven by practical assessment, with a clear path from foundation to leadership. My long-term ambition is training strong enough that other properties send their people to learn from it.
03Proposal
Measure it
A proposed framework, Invisible Excellence, that turns the philosophy into measures any hotel can use: reliability, financial intelligence, resource responsibility, people and capability, guest experience.
04Concept
Build the tools
Practical engineering intelligence: capital investment calculators, performance dashboards, digital maintenance workflows and predictive demonstrations, each clearly labelled for what it is and what it is not.
05In progress
Lead by example
Run departments and pre-openings to the standard I describe, and develop people into greater responsibility. A vision that is not practised is not worth publishing.
06Concept
Share it
Write and speak: case studies, decision journals and commentary, so the thinking is open to challenge and useful beyond one employer.
The Invisible Excellence Framework

How I would measure it

A framework I am proposing, not an established industry standard. For every measure the plan is to define the baseline, period, method, owner and data source, and to normalise energy and water for occupancy and conditions.

Pillar
Possible measures
Reliability
Unplanned downtime, repeat failures, critical asset availability, preventive maintenance effectiveness.
Financial intelligence
Cost per occupied room, lifecycle cost, verified savings, budget variance, return on capital investment.
Resource responsibility
Energy and water intensity, emissions where measurable, waste reduction, equipment efficiency.
People and capability
Competency progression, training effectiveness, internal promotions, quality of work.
Guest experience
Engineering-related complaints, rooms out of order, guest-impacting failures, service recovery.
