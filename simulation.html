What this model assumes

Heat pump performance is modelled from first principles, not from a manufacturer data sheet. Capacity is scaled off a 20 °C ambient / 55 °C water rating point, derating roughly 2.2 % per °C of ambient below that and 0.8 % per °C of leaving water temperature above it. COP is a Carnot fraction (0.45) taken between the evaporator and condenser, which lands around 3.2–3.5 at 20 °C ambient and 2.4–2.7 at 8 °C. Replace these with the manufacturer's published performance curves before relying on any output.

The 22 kW unit is assumed to be the same generation and refrigerant as the new ones. If it is older, R22, or a different make, its real output at 8 °C ambient will be worse than shown here.

Series piping means one common flow through all three machines. Each stage sees the one before it, so the last machine works at the highest lift and the lowest COP. Total output barely changes when you reorder them — the reason to choose an order is which machine you want carrying the high-lift duty, not efficiency.

The two Thermocubes are modelled as one 5000 L stratified column of twelve layers, which is how a pair piped in series behaves. Heat pump return is drawn from the bottom, flow is injected at the top, guests draw off the top, cold makes up at the bottom. Layers are held in stable order each step, so the model will not show cold water sitting above hot.

Guest behaviour is stochastic. Shower start times are sampled from a resort profile weighted to 06:00–09:00 and 18:00–21:00, with durations scattered around your figure. Change the seed for a different set of guests on the same settings — if the plant only passes on one seed, it does not pass.

The failure feedback loop is real. When the top of the store drops toward 40 °C, guests open the hot tap further to hold temperature, so the hot draw rises just as the store is weakest. That is why these systems fail suddenly rather than gradually.

Not modelled: defrost cycles in detail, pipework thermal mass, TMV behaviour, pump failure, scale fouling on the condensers, or laundry and kitchen loads. Add kitchen demand through the basins figure if this plant serves it.

Legionella is flagged on time spent below 50 °C anywhere in the store. It is a flag, not a compliance assessment.
